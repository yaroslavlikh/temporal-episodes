\begin{abstract}
In long multi-party conversations, useful knowledge rarely coincides with a
single self-contained message: states change, decisions get refined, and a late
question is phrased differently from the original discussion. Raw messages
preserve evidentiality but close this semantic gap poorly; free-form summaries
improve access but can lose the author, the time and the per-fact link to the
source. We study \emph{event memory with addressable sources} (\method): every
derived event has its own text for retrieval and immutable pointers to the
originating messages, carrying author, timestamp and a verbatim quote. Events
are built before any question is read, compete with raw messages in a single
ranking, and after retrieval expand back into primary evidence.

\textbf{What is confirmed.} The event layer reliably improves \emph{evidence
delivery}. Across all 2,400 EverMemBench questions, the precision of delivered
gold sources rises by $+3.21\pp$ (95\% CI $[+2.84;+3.59]$, $p<10^{-59}$) while
recall rises simultaneously by $+0.64\pp$ ($[+0.48;+0.80]$). This is not a
trade-off: events deliver \emph{fewer} sources than raw retrieval (9.47 versus
10.00, never more), and still find \emph{more} gold ones ($+0.117$ per question,
$[+0.083;+0.151]$). The direction is positive in all nine official categories.
The effect replicates on SocialMemBench ($+0.90\pp$ precision for flat memory,
$p<10^{-31}$) and dominates query-side expansion: at statistically
indistinguishable recall, events deliver $+6.68\pp$ ($[+5.06;+8.27]$) denser
evidence than matched HyDE.

\textbf{What is not confirmed.} None of these gains converts into answer
quality. Across 2,400 questions micro accuracy moves by $+0.38\pp$
(CI $[-0.8;+1.6]$). A registered confirmatory check on the 2,100 questions
outside the discovery slice does not support the link: where events deliver
strictly better evidence ($n=650$), the accuracy gain is $+0.77\pp$
($p=.649$). The only nominally significant accuracy gain sits on a post hoc
selected Temporal Duration slice and does not survive a Holm correction over
nine categories; the endpoint whose slice and analysis were both fixed in
advance (Group primary, $n=150$) is negative.

\textbf{Contribution.} We report a \emph{dissociation}: in multi-party
conversational memory with a strong dense raw baseline, a derived representation
measurably improves what reaches the generator, and this is not the binding
constraint on answer quality. Both halves of the claim are measured on the same
sealed runs, and the evidence endpoint is deterministic (0 discrepancies out of
300 under a stochastic rerun) whereas accuracy flips on 4\% of questions. The
practical conclusion: addressable evidence is almost free (\$1.92 per domain of
ingestion against \$32.10 for Hindsight, 0.41 GB, $+2$ ms), but one should not
expect an accuracy gain from it on current benchmarks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Conversational memory faces a conflict between two goals. Retrieval is best
served by a self-contained description: ``Alexey cancelled his attendance
because he is leaving.'' Verification needs the original material: who exactly
wrote this, when, and in what words. If the history is replaced by summaries the
answer becomes more accessible, but the boundary between evidence and
interpretation disappears. If only messages are kept, the proof survives, but a
late question has to guess anew all the elliptical, scattered and revised
messages.

The problem is especially visible in a group. The author of a message, the owner
of an opinion and the subject of a statement can be three different people.
Several topics run at once, and positional proximity in the stream does not mean
membership in the same line of conversation. Finally, states evolve: ``I will be
at the meeting'' becomes ``I have left town'' a week later. A flat profile will
easily either keep both versions as current, or erase the first one and deprive
the system of any way to explain the change.

We propose to treat derived memory not as a new source of truth but as an
\emph{index over evidence}. In our implementation, \method{}, an event contains
(i) text optimized for semantic retrieval; (ii) a type, a viewpoint owner, a
subject and an observed time; (iii) an immutable set of source IDs and literal
quotes. Events compete with raw turns in a single vector top-$k$, and the
selected derived documents expand back into messages. Episodes are an optional
layer: they link revision, reaffirmation and augmentation, but do not replace
events or their sources.

This work answers three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item is the full system competitive against published memory systems within a
consistent evaluation harness;
\item does the event representation produce an effect beyond a larger context
volume, message order and strong raw retrieval;
\item does the effect transfer across different kinds of multi-party memory
tasks, and does episode linking justify its additional complexity.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A two-view memory unit in which the retrieval abstraction is separated
from immutable raw evidence, and author and time are preserved all the way to
the answer.
\item Query-independent construction and unified retrieval over heterogeneous
memory units with deterministic provenance expansion.
\item A full evaluation on EverMemBench, SocialMemBench and GroupMemBench with
recorded cost, size, latency and frozen artifacts.
\item A mechanistic set of controls separating amount of text, chronological
order, event representation and episode linking, including an honest negative
result for linking.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Long-term memory for agents.}
Generative Agents introduce an experience stream, retrieval and reflection
\citep{park2023}; MemGPT manages several context tiers \citep{packer2023}. Mem0
extracts and consolidates durable facts, and its graph variant adds relations
\citep{chhikara2025mem0}. Zep/Graphiti stores a temporally-aware knowledge graph
\citep{rasmussen2025zep}; A-MEM organizes notes dynamically \citep{xu2025amem};
Hindsight separates facts, experience, summaries and beliefs
\citep{latimer2025hindsight}. HippoRAG uses graph association and personalized
PageRank \citep{gutierrez2024hipporag}.

Our goal is narrower than building universal memory: bounded multi-party chat
with identifiable participants. The novelty is not in the mere use of an LLM or
a vector, nor in ranking raw and derived documents jointly, which is partly
anticipated by the collapsed-tree mode of RAPTOR \citep{sarthi2024raptor}, but
in the joint construction of a \emph{retrieval representation + immutable
evidence address} with speaker and time attribution, and in a controlled
analysis of when it helps.

\paragraph{Benchmarks and attribution.}
LoCoMo and LongMemEval evaluate multi-session recall
\citep{maharana2024,wu2024}. EverMemBench adds long multi-party and multi-group
projects \citep{hu2026}; GroupMemBench controls reply structure, speaker
conditioning and implicit links \citep{yang2026}; SocialMemBench measures facts,
norms, exceptions and attribution in social groups \citep{owolabi2026}. RAG and
ALCE separate retrieval usefulness from citation quality
\citep{lewis2020,gao2023}. Likewise, we do not treat a valid source pointer as
proof that a derived claim is true.

\paragraph{Derived units of indexing.}
The closest class of work builds a derived representation over the corpus before
any query. Dense X Retrieval indexes propositions --- atomic self-contained
statements extracted from passages --- and shows that this granularity beats
passages and sentences on open-domain QA \citep{chen2023densex}. RAPTOR
recursively clusters and summarizes chunks into a tree, and in its main mode
(collapsed tree) flattens it into a single layer and ranks all nodes at once by
cosine --- both the original leaves and the generated summaries
\citep{sarthi2024raptor}; by their own measurements, between 18.5\% and 57\% of
the returned nodes are not leaves.

Let us say it plainly: the idea of a single pool of raw and derived documents is
not new and is partly anticipated by the collapsed tree. Our difference lies
elsewhere, in the nature of the unit. Both works are methods over a document
corpus: their derived units have no author and no time, and the link to the
source has the granularity of a passage or a leaf, not of a per-fact address.
RAPTOR itself reports that about 4\% of its summaries contain small
hallucinations, and its structure does not allow a specific statement to be
traced back to a specific support. For multi-party correspondence this is not
enough: the answer must name who said it and when, and the owner of an opinion
may coincide neither with the author of the message nor with its subject. Our
derived text is therefore accompanied by an immutable set of (source ID, literal
quote) pairs checked by a validator, and by separate owner and subject fields.

The conclusions differ too. Dense X reports a broad gain for a fine derived
unit; on multi-party chat we obtain a narrow one --- events help where evidence
is scattered in time, and do not help on average (§\ref{sec:mech}). Plausible
reasons for the difference are a different domain and a markedly stronger dense
raw baseline than passage retrieval over Wikipedia.

\paragraph{What representations are compared against.}
Query rewriting and HyDE can reduce the lexical gap on the question side
\citep{gao2023hyde}; event memory does this on the corpus side, once, before QA,
and preserves the link to evidence. We compare both mechanisms on Temporal
Duration: HyDE does not change accuracy relative to \raw{} either at top-10 or
after matching the token budget and chronological order.

\section{Problem statement}

Let $M=(m_1,\ldots,m_n)$ be the history of a single bounded conversation space.
A message $m_i=(id_i,g_i,a_i,t_i,x_i)$ contains the space, the author, the time
and the text. A builder $B$ receives only $M$ and creates memory $\mathcal{D}$
before QA opens. For a query $q$, retrieval selects documents
$R_k(q,\mathcal{D})$, after which the generator answers from the expanded
context $C_q$.

We distinguish three properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{pointer integrity}: the source ID exists, and the quote is
literally contained in it;
\item \textbf{semantic support}: the source text actually entails the derived
description;
\item \textbf{answer correctness}: the generator used the delivered context
correctly.
\end{enumerate}
A mechanical check guarantees only the first. The second requires an entailment
or human audit, the third is measured by benchmark scoring.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The immutable event}

An event has the form
\[
e=(z,o,s,k,r,\tau,P,L),
\]
where $z$ is the self-contained index text, $o$ the viewpoint owner, $s$ the
subject, $k$ the event type, $r$ the temporal mode, $\tau$ the observed time,
$P$ the evidence and $L$ the local context. Evidence consists of pairs
$(id_i, quote_i)$; time is derived from the real timestamps of the sources.

Separating $o$ from $s$ prevents naive attribution collapse: ``A called B
unreliable'' is stored as A's opinion about B, not as an unconditional fact
about B. The types cover state, opinion, decision, commitment, relation,
exception and stable pattern. A conversational reaction with no long-term
content may produce no event; a cap of at most two events per turn applies.

\subsection{Query-independent extraction and validation}

The history is split only along natural benchmark session boundaries: in
EverMemBench by day and group; in GroupMemBench by channel/phase with blocks of
up to 40 messages. Forced structured output permits an empty list. A
deterministic validator then removes invented IDs, missing quotes, content-free
values and invalid enums. Local context may help resolve a pronoun, but does not
automatically become evidence.

\subsection{Optional versioned episodes}

An episode $E_j=((e_1,\rho_1),\ldots,(e_t,\rho_t))$ is an append-only line where
$\rho\in\{\texttt{create},\texttt{revise},\texttt{reaffirm},\texttt{augment}\}$.
For a new event, candidates are searched only within the conversation network.
The score is
\[
S(e,E)=0.6\cos(\phi(z_e),\phi(A_E))+0.4\cos(\phi(z_e),\phi(z_{last(E)})).
\]
The three best episodes go to an LLM resolver; when confidence is insufficient a
new episode is created. The index keeps a hot view of the last five events, but
cold events and evidence are not deleted. A false split is cheaper here than a
false merge: split events can still enter retrieval independently, whereas an
erroneous merge systematically brings in extraneous sources.

\subsection{Unified retrieval and provenance expansion}

A raw turn and a derived document share a common interface
\[
d=(id,\ kind,\ index\_text,\ render\_text,\ source\_ids).
\]
The question vector is compared against a single pool; the top-$10$ may contain
both kinds. After ranking, each event/episode expands into unique raw turns. The
generator sees the derived text as a navigational hint and the dated,
speaker-grounded sources as the basis of the answer.

This differs from ``show all the profiles'': derived memory is filtered by the
same relevance criterion as raw history. Static profiles and a description of
the personal chat are not used in the experiments.

The main paired baseline is a strong dense \raw{}, not a weak keyword-only
search. The \events{} and \episodes{} conditions add derived documents to the
same top-$10$. For Temporal Duration we additionally fix the token count, the
set of expanded raw sources and the chronological order; HyDE generates a

documents. All variants use identical official answer/judge prompts inside the
corresponding benchmark adapter.

EverMemBench uses GPT-4.1-mini for answering, Gemini-3-Flash-preview as the
open-answer judge, text-embedding-3-large for retrieval and
gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18 for memory construction. The answer and judge models,
their prompts and the exact multiple-choice scoring are taken from the official
harness; providers are pinned (OpenAI and Google AI Studio respectively) with
fallbacks disabled --- that is, our rows and the published ones do not differ by
the judge model. All calls run at \texttt{temperature}${}=0$. SocialMemBench
follows the GPT-4o-mini official-compatible harness. GroupMemBench uses the
GPT-5 of the official run; the provider's model version was not pinned by date,
so byte-level reproduction of these numbers is not guaranteed. Comparisons
across benchmarks are not to be read as comparisons of models.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:stats}

We report micro accuracy, macro accuracy over question types, evidence
recall/precision from the intersection of expanded source IDs with gold anchors
--- the reference spans released by the benchmark, that is, the messages marked
as the support of the answer --- paired differences, question bootstrap 95\% CI
and exact McNemar. For the five Ever projects a cluster/sign-flip analysis is
computed in addition. Evidence metrics are not entailment and not the quality of
user-facing citations.

Memory and inputs are stored in JSONL/SQLite, sealed with SHA-256 and reused
across controls. Temporal Duration was selected after inspecting the full
breakdown and is marked exploratory everywhere. The family of multiple
comparisons for the mechanistic claim is the nine official EverMemBench
categories; we report the Holm correction with $m=9$ next to the nominal $p$
values for it (§\ref{sec:mech}). A separate power ceiling applies: with five
project clusters the minimum attainable two-sided sign-flip value is
$2/2^5=.0625$, so cluster-level confirmation is unreachable on these data under
any outcome.

\paragraph{Two endpoints and their resolving power.}
We report results at two levels, and they differ not in importance but in
measurability. \emph{Accuracy} passes through the generator and the judge;
\emph{evidence recall/precision} are computed mechanically from expanded source
IDs and gold anchors and depend on neither. The difference is visible
empirically: on a repeated run of one and the same condition over the same 300
questions, accuracy produced 12 flipped verdicts out of 300 (4.0\%; 6 in each
direction, a net shift of exactly $0.00\pp$), whereas the evidence metrics
agreed \emph{on all 300 questions}. Accuracy is a noisy proxy with ceilings on
easy categories; the evidence endpoint is deterministic and resolves effects
that accuracy at the available $n$ cannot resolve in principle. We therefore
present evidence as the main measured result and accuracy as the question of
whether it converts (§\ref{sec:frontier}), and deliberately do not substitute
one for the other.

\subsection{Transfer across benchmarks}

SocialMemBench gives MeanQ .358 for \raw{} and .368 for \episodes{}: $+0.010$,
network-bootstrap 95\% CI $[-.008;+.028]$. The result is practically identical
to the published full-context .369 and above the listed benchmark memory rows
(best Subject-Mem .321), but there is no significant gain of its own.

On accuracy, Social is thus a draw. But at the level of evidence delivery the
same run gives a resolved positive effect, and this is the only cross-benchmark
transfer the work managed to establish at all. Paired over 1,031 questions
against \raw{}: flat memory $+0.90\pp$ precision (CI $[+0.70;+1.11]$,
$p<10^{-31}$), versioned $+0.86\pp$ ($[+0.65;+1.06]$), episodes $+0.34\pp$
($[+0.17;+0.51]$) at $+0.99\pp$ recall ($[+0.16;+1.82]$). The magnitude is
markedly smaller than on Ever ($+3.21\pp$), and the reason is transparent: the
base evidence recall on Social is 67.5\% against 8.1\% on Ever, so there is
almost nothing left to improve. The direction, however, reproduces on a second
dataset, with a different model and a different harness.

On the pre-registered Group primary (Finance+Technology, knowledge
update+temporal, $n=150$) the original protocol result was
.340$\rightarrow$.300. A targeted amendment repeated only the 102 empty
generations with the same sealed prompts and a larger output budget; 102/102
were restored, and the remaining 1,388 answers were left unchanged. After the
repair \raw{} reaches .373 and \episodes{} .333: the delta stays at $-4.0\pp$,
CI $[-9.3;+1.3]$, $p=.210$. Over all 745 QA the result is
.431$\rightarrow$.415, $-1.6\pp$, CI $[-4.0;+0.8]$, $p=.246$. The negative
direction is therefore not an artifact of the empty outputs. The repair cost
\$2.69; 94\% of its output tokens went to hidden reasoning, which confirms the
cause of the original failure.

It matters that both the slice and the analysis were fixed in advance here: the
Finance+Technology domains, the knowledge update+temporal types, $n=150$ and the
statistics were written into the protocol before the run. Unlike the temporal
effect (§\ref{sec:mech}), where the advance fixing covers only the endpoint and
not the choice of slice, this result is protected from selection on both sides
--- and it is negative. The asymmetry of status between the negative and the
positive results of this work does not point in our favour, and we consider it
necessary to say so directly.

\warningbox{\textbf{Boundary of generalization.} The method is not a universal
replacement for raw retrieval. On Social it is neutral on average; on Group the
current version may displace useful speaker-conditioned raw turns even after the
repair. This negative transfer is part of the result, not a hidden failure.}

\section{The event representation: what it changes and what it does not}
\label{sec:mech}

This section is split by endpoint. First we show the effect that is measured
directly and confirmed on the whole dataset: events change which evidence
reaches the generator (§\ref{sec:delivery}). Then, where and how this is
reflected in accuracy (§\ref{sec:temporal}), and finally the registered check of
whether the two are linked at all (§\ref{sec:frontier}). To anticipate: the
first is confirmed, the third is not.

\label{sec:delivery}

\events{}$-$\raw{}, paired: precision $+3.21\pp$ (95\% CI $[+2.84;+3.59]$,
Wilcoxon $p=2.9\cdot10^{-60}$, 855 questions better against 255 worse), recall
$+0.64\pp$ ($[+0.48;+0.80]$, $p=4.6\cdot10^{-13}$). The sign is positive in all
nine official categories, and the CI excludes zero in eight out of nine
(appendix, Table~\ref{tab:evid-cats}).

\paragraph{This is not a mechanical artifact of volume.}
Precision could have risen on its own if events simply delivered less text. The
check gives the opposite: \events{} deliver fewer sources than \raw{} (9.47
versus 10.00) and \emph{never} more --- while finding more gold ones: $2.245$
against $2.129$ hits per question, a delta of $+0.117$ (CI
$[+0.083;+0.151]$). Less context, more evidence --- dominance, not a trade-off;
on 449 questions it is strict. \episodes{} behave differently: they buy recall
with volume ($+2.68\pp$ recall at $+2.4$ sources), and their precision grows
more weakly ($+1.16\pp$). This is the first place in the work where linking and
separate events differ measurably --- but they differ in cost, not in usefulness
for the answer (§\ref{sec:episodes}).

\paragraph{Query-side expansion does not reproduce this.}
Matched HyDE on Temporal Duration, at aligned budget and order, gives recall
statistically indistinguishable from \events{} ($-1.01\pp$, CI
$[-2.20;+0.17]$), but a markedly sparser context: \events{}$-$HyDE in precision
is $+6.68\pp$ (CI $[+5.06;+8.27]$, $p=9.3\cdot10^{-16}$). HyDE is not useless in
itself --- against token-matched \raw{} it gives $+1.18\pp$ precision and
$+1.61\pp$ recall --- it is dominated. Expansion on the query side and
normalization on the corpus side move different coordinates.

\resultbox{\textbf{What is established.} The derived layer improves evidence
delivery reliably, over the whole dataset, in every category, with replication
on a second benchmark and dominance over query-side expansion. This is measured
by a deterministic metric that depends neither on the generator, nor on the
judge, nor on the choice of slice. \emph{Whether this gain remains useful} is a
separate question, and the answer to it is negative (§\ref{sec:frontier}).}

\subsection{Accuracy: where the effect is visible and why this is the weak spot}
\label{sec:temporal}

We analyse Temporal Duration as the slice with the largest \emph{relative} gain
of the full Ever breakdown: \raw{} 12.3\%, \episodes{} 20.0\%, that is
$+7.7\pp$ and $+63\%$ over the base (Table~\ref{tab:ever-slices}). In absolute
points, a slightly larger shift is given by Style
($30.1\%\rightarrow38.1\%$, $+8.0\pp$), but at a three times higher base and
with no hypothesis about a mechanism; for \events{} without controls, Temporal
Duration is the largest in absolute points as well ($+4.0\pp$ against $+3.4\pp$
for Style). We do not explain the gain on Style and do not include it in the
claims of this work (§\ref{sec:limits}). The choice of slice itself remains post
hoc. But derived documents expand into several messages, so the generator sees
17.4 sources on average instead of 10. To separate representation from volume,
we gave \raw{} the same token budget; then we separately aligned chronological
order and replaced linked episodes with independent events. A matched HyDE
control tests whether query-side expansion can produce the same effect without
building event memory.

At equal token budget and order, \events{} reach 19.3\% against 14.0\% for

also gives 14.0\%; the difference \events{}$-$HyDE is $+5.33\pp$, CI
$[+0.33;+10.33]$, nominal $p=.0479$ (37 wins against 21 losses). The direction
is positive in all five projects, but with five clusters the minimum attainable
sign-flip is $p=.0625$. Over all 2,100 questions outside Temporal Duration,
\events{} give $-0.14\pp$, CI $[-1.33;+1.10]$, $p=.877$. The effect is not
explained by a simple ``derived memory is better everywhere''; it concentrates
where scattered temporal boundaries have to be found.

The mechanistic picture does not reduce to a single retrieval metric. \raw{}
top-10 already has 29.6\% precision of expanded gold sources --- no lower than
\events{} (29.3\%) --- but at 12.7\% recall it yields only 12.3\% accuracy. To
raise recall, raw retrieval has to widen the window: token-matched \raw{}
reaches 19.1\% recall, precision falls to 21.5\%, and accuracy grows only to
14.0\%. HyDE shifts the same curve further into recall (20.7\%) at 22.6\%
precision --- and accuracy does not change at all. \events{} hold both
coordinates at once: recall 19.7\% at the level of token-matched \raw{} and
precision 29.3\% at the level of \raw{} top-10, which is what 19.3\%
corresponds to.

On this slice events hold both coordinates at once, whereas raw retrieval gets
them one at a time: two conditions with almost the same recall (19.1\% and
19.7\%) give 14.0\% and 19.3\%, and two conditions with almost the same
precision (29.6\% and 29.3\%) give 12.3\% and 19.3\%. HyDE fits the same
picture: it moves recall only and does not reproduce the event gain.

An explanation suggests itself --- ``accuracy follows the recall--precision
frontier of delivered evidence''. We tested it and did not confirm it
(§\ref{sec:frontier}); the explanation itself remains a description of four rows
of Table~\ref{tab:controls}, not a mechanism. This is still a post hoc discovery
slice over five project clusters; the project-level sign-flip is $p=.1875$, and
288/300 verdicts agreed in a stochastic rerun.

\paragraph{Correction for multiplicity.}
Temporal Duration was selected from nine official categories after inspecting
the full breakdown, so the family is $m=9$. The first Holm step compares the
smallest $p$ of the family against $\alpha/9=.0056$; our $.0402$ and $.0479$
exceed it, so the procedure stops and no comparison survives the correction
(Bonferroni equivalents $.36$ and $.43$). The remaining eight $p$ values are not
needed for this conclusion. Together with the power ceiling ($.0625$ at five
clusters, §\ref{sec:stats}) this means the effect has no confirmatory
statistical status either at the question level or at the project level.

\paragraph{What exactly was pre-registered.}
The endpoint of the matched-HyDE comparison was fixed before any outcomes were
obtained, but the slice itself was not. Fixing the analysis in advance does not
protect against selection of the slice, so registration here covers only the
second half of the procedure. We read the temporal result as
hypothesis-generating: it is stated narrowly enough for an independent
confirmatory replication to refute it, and we do not present it as a confirmed
effect.

\subsection{Does the gain convert into the answer: a registered check}
\label{sec:frontier}

An explanation that only ever sees the slice on which it was found cannot turn
out to be wrong. We therefore registered a prediction before the computation: if
the frontier is a mechanism, it must also describe the 2,100 questions where
events do not help. The protocol, the reading rule and the power ceilings were
fixed in advance; the check is fully offline and uses per-question fields of
already sealed runs, with no new generations.

Each question outside Temporal Duration is assigned to one stratum by how the
delivered evidence changed in the transition \raw{}$\rightarrow$\events{}:
\textbf{A} --- events dominate (recall and precision no lower, at least one
strictly higher), \textbf{B} --- \raw{} dominates, \textbf{C} --- a mixed case
or equality. The prediction: the accuracy delta is positive in A, negative in B
and indistinguishable from zero in C.

The prediction was not confirmed. In stratum A, where events deliver strictly
better evidence, the gain is only $+0.77\pp$ at $p=.649$. The direction in B is
negative, as predicted, and C does indeed contain zero, but the main condition
of the reading rule is not met. At the category level, the sign of the delta
agrees with the direction of dominance in 1 case out of 5 ($p=.375$).

The categorical correlation, unlike the stratification, turned out to be
\emph{metric-dependent}, and we report this as it is. The correlation between
the gain in evidence F1 and the gain in accuracy over eight categories is
$\rho=-0.07$ ($p=.87$), but the same correlation computed on the gain in
evidence \emph{precision} is $\rho=+0.63$ over nine categories ($p=.07$) and
$\rho=+0.57$ over eight without the discovery slice ($p=.14$). At $n=9$ the
critical value is $0.68$, so neither quantity is resolved, and the choice
between them cannot be made from the data: F1 mixes recall and precision, which
move differently for \events{} and \episodes{} (§\ref{sec:delivery}). We
therefore do not claim that the link is absent at the category level --- we
claim that it is not resolved on nine points, whereas the pre-registered
stratified test on 2,100 questions does not confirm it. The conclusion of this
work rests on the second, not the first.

What the check does \emph{not} show. There are few discordant pairs --- 77 in
stratum A, 23 in B, 67 in C --- because a change of context rarely flips an
answer. At 77 pairs the minimum detectable effect in A is $\approx2.9\pp$, so
the result excludes a large effect but does not resolve a small one. This is an
absence of confirmation, not a demonstrated zero.

The conclusion is taken by the rule written down in advance: the frontier
remains a description of the discovery slice and loses the status of an
explanation. An improvement in delivered evidence does not by itself imply an
improvement in the answer, and why events win specifically on Temporal Duration
remains unknown.

Together with §\ref{sec:delivery} this gives the main result of this work, and
it is two-sided. The gain in evidence delivery is real, large by statistical
standards and reproducible; there is no gain in the answer, and the link between
them is not confirmed by a direct check. We call this a \emph{dissociation}: in
multi-party conversational memory with a strong dense raw baseline, retrieval
quality stops being the binding constraint long before it becomes good. Both
halves are measured on the same sealed runs, so the asymmetry of status here is
not rhetorical: what is confirmed is what is easier to measure, not what is more
convenient for the method.

\subsection{What episodes add over events}
\label{sec:episodes}

New symmetric controls remove the advantage of linking: \episodes{} chrono
18.3\%, \events{} chrono 19.3\%; the difference is $-1.0\pp$, CI
$[-5.0;+3.33]$, $p=.755$. In the previously sealed comparison of ranked episodes
20.0\% against events chrono 19.3\%, the difference was only $+0.67\pp$,
$p=.888$.

We separately note an inconsistency that we do not explain: chronological order
helps \raw{} ($+1.7\pp$) and \events{} ($+2.3\pp$), but for \episodes{} it is
harmful ($20.0\rightarrow18.3$). All three shifts are single-digit question
counts at $n=300$, and we do not treat them as distinguishable; this is more an
estimate of the scale of noise than three different effects.

This is an important negative result. A versioned chain is useful for inspection
and can connect specific changes, but its average contribution to retrieval is
not separable from a set of individual events. Good retrieval can survive a
false split by delivering both records independently, so complicating the linker
is not obliged to improve the endpoint.

\subsection{Reproducibility as part of the method}

Memory construction finishes before any QA is read; snapshots and input
manifests are sealed with SHA-256. Model calls are cached by content and by
prompt/schema version. On interruption the process continues from the last
completed unit. Derived records pass a repeated source-ID check; experiments
A/B2 were run over one frozen memory without re-extraction.

These details do not improve the benchmark score directly, but they protect the
main claim from oracle leakage and make it possible to distinguish a change of
method from repeated sampling.

\section{Error analysis}
\label{sec:errors}

\paragraph{Extraction misses.}
If no event is created, the system reduces to \raw{}. Forced structured output
substantially raises recall relative to voluntary tool calling, but does not
guarantee completeness.

\paragraph{Attribution and semantic overreach.}
A literally present quote may support only part of a description; a paraphrase
may mistake the author of a message for the owner of an opinion. Therefore 100\%
quote validity means integrity of the address, not 100\% correct facts.

\paragraph{False split and false merge.}
Free-form subject/facet wording splits one line into several episodes. Retrieval
often survives a split by finding both records. A merge is more dangerous: it
brings irrelevant context into every future request. A conservative resolver
naturally prefers NEW, which shortens the chains but protects precision.

\paragraph{Context displacement.}
A derived hit occupies a slot in the top-$k$ and sometimes displaces a useful
raw turn. This is a plausible explanation of the neutral/negative transfer
outside the temporal slice and especially in the speaker-conditioned
GroupMemBench.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The main mechanism result was found post hoc; all of its controls use the same
300 questions and are not an independent replication. After the Holm correction
over the nine official categories it is not significant ($\alpha/9=.0056$
against the nominal $.0402$ and $.0479$), and the five Ever projects do not
permit cluster-level significance under any outcome (minimum attainable
$p=.0625$). Meanwhile the endpoint whose slice and analysis were both fixed in
advance (Group primary) is negative. The positive conclusion of this work
therefore remains hypothesis-generating and requires a confirmatory replication
on an independent slice. Overall accuracy stays around 50\%, and temporal below
20\%.

Let us bound the confirmed result symmetrically as well. The evidence metric
measures \emph{delivery}, not usefulness: a gold anchor in the context does not
mean the generator used it, and a rise in precision does not mean the derived
text is semantically faithful. The absolute magnitude is modest --- $+0.117$
gold hits per question --- and a base recall of 8.1\% means that more than 90\%
of gold evidence is retrieved by none of the conditions; we are moving a small
share of a small share. Finally, gold anchors are released by the benchmark, and
all conclusions inherit their completeness.

Let us bound the null separately. Memory is built by a cheap extractor
(gpt-4o-mini), so the conclusion ``there is no universal event gain'' holds for
this extraction quality, and the upper bound under a strong extractor is not
measured. The cap of ``at most two events per turn'' and the weights $0.6/0.4$
in $S(e,E)$ were chosen a priori and are not ablated. The frontier explanation
does not survive its own confirmatory check (§\ref{sec:frontier}), so the
observed advantage on Temporal Duration remains without a mechanism.

The source pointer is mechanically valid, but a full human entailment audit has
not been completed. Review sheets exist, but are not fully annotated and are not
used as a published metric. The HyDE control is complete, but uses the same 300
exploratory questions and is not an independent replication. The targeted
GroupMemBench amendment restored all 102 initially empty outputs without
changing the remaining 1,388 answers and verdicts. It remains a post-protocol
analysis, not an official leaderboard submission. While filling the repair cache
in parallel, one shared payload was called twice because of a missing
inter-process lock; the extracted final answers coincided, and the duplicate
changed no metric. Costs depend on prices as of 12 September 2026, on caches and
on the chosen APIs; latency was measured locally.

Evidence metrics were not computed for GroupMemBench: the runner stored only the
verdict. This is the only dataset with a stable negative transfer, so it is
exactly where the dissociation should have been tested a third time, and exactly
where we did not test it. The computation is offline and requires no new
generations --- the expanded source IDs for both conditions are already sealed
--- but at the time of writing it has not been performed, and the displacement
hypothesis from §\ref{sec:errors} remains untested.

Finally, the method does not propose a new foundation model and does not solve
open-domain or multimodal memory. It assumes a bounded conversation network and
stable participant identifiers; upstream transcription and identity resolution
are outside the central evaluation.

\section{Conclusion}

We investigated a simple invariant: derived memory should help retrieval without
substituting itself for the proof. \method{} stores a self-contained event
representation together with immutable addresses of raw turns, ranks raw and
derived documents in one pool, and expands retrieved memory back into speaker-
and time-grounded evidence.

The first half of the invariant holds, and it is measured more reliably than
anything else in this work. Events improve evidence delivery over all 2,400
questions ($+3.21\pp$ precision at $+0.64\pp$ recall), while delivering less
text, not more; the effect is positive in all nine categories, reproduces on
SocialMemBench and dominates matched HyDE. This is measured by a deterministic
metric that depends neither on the generator nor on the judge.

The second half does not hold. None of these gains carries over into answer
quality: across all questions accuracy does not move, and a registered check on
the 2,100 questions outside the discovery slice does not confirm a link between
better evidence and better answers. The only nominally significant accuracy gain
was found post hoc and does not survive correction; the endpoint whose slice and
analysis were both fixed in advance is negative. Social transfers neutrally,
Group negatively even after the targeted repair.

We consider this dissociation the main result, not a failure. It means that in
multi-party conversational memory with a strong dense raw baseline, improving
retrieval stops paying off before retrieval becomes good: at a base recall of
8\% the bottleneck is no longer there. The practical consequence for an engineer
is direct --- \emph{store the evidence first, then build a retrieval abstraction
over it}: addressability is almost free (\$1.92 per domain, 0.41 GB, $+2$ ms)
and survives errors in the derived layer, but an accuracy gain from it should
not be planned for on today's benchmarks. The consequence for a researcher is
about what to measure: accuracy at these sample sizes does not resolve effects
that the evidence endpoint resolves by tens of orders of magnitude, and work
that reports on accuracy alone is likely to see neither its effect nor the
absence of one.

EverMemBench code \texttt{e10b3d52f0e4}, data \texttt{a6b210a32248};
GroupMemBench \texttt{e2682e01ff49}. The figure script hashes the input

\paragraph{Which Social run is canonical.}
Two SocialMemBench pipelines are present in the artifacts, and they must not be
mixed. The canonical one is
\path{socialmembench_temporal_episodes_official_harness_v1}:
\texttt{text-embedding-3-small}, one cosine top-10 per condition, an identical
code path for answer/judge/packing; every number in this work comes from it, and
its RAW/FLAT/VERSIONED rows are the unmodified results of the frozen run of
2026-09-11. The second, \path{socialmembench_temporal_episodes_full_v1}, is an
earlier hybrid (local \texttt{MiniLM}, raw top-12 + episode top-12, a 400-word
budget); its \raw{} gives an evidence recall of 48.5\% against 67.5\% for the
canonical one. Comparing a condition from one run against \raw{} from the other
produces plausible and entirely false numbers; we note this here because both
directories are distributed together.

\paragraph{Recomputation of the evidence endpoint.}
All the paired numbers of §\ref{sec:delivery} and of
Table~\ref{tab:evid-cats} are reproduced offline by the script
\path{research/evidence_endpoint_analysis.py}, which reads only sealed runs,
makes no API calls and writes
\path{research/figures/evidence_endpoint_results.json}; the manuscript takes its
numbers from there. In the public repository the same values, together with the
registered frontier check of §\ref{sec:frontier}, are recomputed from the
published per-question files by \path{analysis/verify_evidence_endpoint.py},
which compares each one against the value printed here and fails if any of them
disagrees.

\paragraph{Admissible formulations.}
One may speak of the competitiveness of the full system in an adapter
evaluation, of a confirmed improvement in \emph{evidence delivery} over the
whole dataset and its replication on SocialMemBench, of dominance over matched
HyDE in the density of delivered evidence, of a nominal temporal signal in
accuracy, and of provenance as addressability of the source. One may not call
the result an official SOTA, a proven universal gain, semantically verified
facts, or a proven benefit of episode linking. Nor may one claim that events win
\emph{because} they improve the recall--precision frontier of delivered
evidence: the registered check did not confirm this (§\ref{sec:frontier}). The
confirmed delivery result may not be carried over to answer quality: that is
exactly the implication this work tests and does not confirm. An improvement in
precision may not be called semantic faithfulness of the derived text, and it
may not be extended to GroupMemBench, where evidence metrics were not computed.
